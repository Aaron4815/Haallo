# Executive Business-Plan Summary

## Company thesis

Physical AI Data Germany is an early-stage B2B company designed to turn real human work demonstrations into **robot-ready, auditable data products** for robotics companies, system integrators and industrial automation teams.

The initial market entry is not a generic video marketplace. The company starts with **paid, task-specific data operations**:

1. Feasibility and task-design sprint
2. Capture pilot
3. Production data campaign
4. Recurring data operations
5. Later, carefully licensed reusable data assets

## Customer problem

Robot teams often have strong models and hardware but still lack enough real-world, task-specific data for:

- changing object positions and environments,
- deformable or flexible components,
- rare failure states,
- recovery behavior,
- calibrated 3D interaction data,
- legally documented use and provenance.

Building an internal capture and quality operation consumes engineering time and introduces legal, operational and information-security risks.

## Solution

The planned service covers:

- task decomposition and data specification,
- capture-protocol design,
- participant and location operations,
- synchronized multi-view/RGB-D recording,
- optional hand, tool and object trajectories,
- failure and recovery data,
- annotation and task-phase segmentation,
- privacy filtering,
- quality assurance and acceptance reporting,
- customer-specific or compatible robotics-data export,
- rights, provenance and deletion documentation.

## Initial niche

Flexible industrial manipulation:

- cable routing,
- connector insertion,
- hoses and clips,
- deformable components,
- machine tending with variable states,
- inspection and corrective actions.

## Commercial model

### Entry product: 10-day feasibility sprint

Planned introductory price for initial design partners: **EUR 4,900 net**.

Deliverables:

- concrete task and modality specification,
- variation matrix,
- data schema,
- measurable acceptance criteria,
- rights/provenance concept,
- quality-assurance plan,
- costed scope for a larger capture pilot,
- final go/no-go workshop.

The sprint does not promise model improvement and does not automatically include a production dataset.

### Follow-on products

- Capture pilot: approximately EUR 20,000–50,000 depending on scope
- Production campaign: approximately EUR 60,000–300,000+
- Recurring updates and data operations: contract-based

All prices are planning assumptions and must be validated through real proposals and customer demand.

## Operating model

The founder is responsible for business development, finance and field operations. A senior technical lead must be contractually secured before the first production project. Variable resources such as demonstrators, annotation and project logistics are commissioned against signed work.

The company uses milestone billing to protect working capital. Hardware and hiring follow evidence gates rather than speculative expansion.

## Compliance principles

- no continuous or covert employee monitoring,
- voluntary, time-limited demonstration sessions,
- standard data minimization and no audio/face capture unless required,
- separate identity and dataset storage,
- project-specific rights and retention,
- no employee scoring, emotion recognition or hidden secondary use,
- documented customer acceptance and data provenance.

## Financial planning

The detailed financial model currently uses a staged financing approach. The larger bank scenario assumes a combination of founder equity and debt, but it is **not ready for submission** until founder funds, availability, technical leadership and customer evidence are documented.

The current recommended stage is lean validation: prove the problem, secure a technical lead and obtain a paid sprint before major hardware or full-scale financing.

## Collaboration request

We are seeking teams with one concrete task and one measurable data gap. A useful first conversation should answer:

1. What task should the robot perform?
2. What data is currently missing?
3. Are human demonstrations, object-centric trajectories, teleoperation or robot actions required?
4. Which benchmark decides whether the data helps?
5. Can a small paid sprint be scoped around that decision?

For qualified financing or institutional discussions, the full business plan and editable financial model are available after an initial call.
